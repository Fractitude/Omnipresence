# Pitch 1: Detective (solo or small co-op)

Living idea log for **Omnipresence in the Wired**. Rough is fine. Mark decisions in the log at the bottom.

## Status

Just started. Have: virtual computer (Navi), first-person movement, jack-in from home to the Wired. Missing: a reason to be there.

## Pillars (draft)

1. **The Wired is a place, not a menu.** You walk, you get lost, you get scared. Navi is the door, never the gameplay.
2. **Two worlds, one cause-and-effect.** Real world and Wired are separate maps that change each other.
3. **Identity is the currency.** Being in the Wired costs you something in the real world. Your presence leaks.
4. **Strange over scary.** Dread, melancholy, static, wrongness. Jump scares are cheap; "this shouldn't be here" is the goal.
5. **Nobody explains.** Lain never does. Players piece it together from places, logs and people.

## Core loop candidates

Pick one as the spine. They can be combined.

- **A. Investigator.** Take a case (a missing person, a leaked file, a dead user still logging in). Real world gives clues and tools; the Wired gives answers and danger. Walk, listen, deduce.
- **B. Two-world puzzler.** A door in the Wired opens a door at home. A lamp left on at home lights a dark Wired corridor. Solve by acting in both places.
- **C. Presence as resource.** Each jack-in thins you out: people forget you, photos change, your room drifts. Go deeper for answers, or stay real to stay safe.

## Real world

Small, quiet, specific. Home first. A room that is the same every time, so changes are noticeable.

- Seeds: your room, the family kitchen, a school or office, a street, an arcade or club, a lab.
- The real world should get *emptier* and *odder* as the Wired gets more of you.
- Real-world actions that matter: plug in hardware, take a call, be seen by someone, leave the house, sleep.

## The Wired

Rules differ by place. Each area is a "protocol": its own physics, look, sound, and danger.

- **Seeds:**
  - The Lobby: where everyone lands. Crowded with flickering avatars, no one answers.
  - Server rooms as architecture: endless corridors of humming racks, cables as vines.
  - Dead zones: abandoned sites, frozen chatrooms, last messages looping.
  - The Black Market: criminal corner. Trades in memories, identities, stolen presence.
  - Cyberia-like club: music, safe-ish, people who know things.
  - The Deep: where the Wired stops looking like a network and starts looking like a mind.
- **Criminal corners:** identity theft as a physical act, cloned avatars, "sold" people, hacked real-world devices, cults around a Wired god.
- **Movement ideas:** walking, fast-travel by address, falling between layers, follow a cable.

## Story seeds

Inspired by Lain, not a retelling. Original characters.

- A user who died and keeps showing up. Or a user who was never alive.
- A protocol that rewrites memory: the player's too.
- A group trying to merge the Wired and reality on purpose.
- Someone is selling copies of you.
- The player's home slowly turns out to be in the Wired too.
- Ending shapes: reality wins / the Wired wins / you vanish from both / you become the Wired's memory.

## Navi, in context

Navi is how you *prepare* and *connect*, not how you play. Good uses:

- Boot-up / jack-in ritual (physical: headset, cables, power-down lights).
- Real-world tools: messages that set up a Wired meeting, files you carry in as items.
- NPCs and clues delivered through it, then followed in 3D.
- A Wired app you open *inside* the Wired, as a place you step into.

## Multiplayer (decision needed)

Project supports 64 players. Solo, co-op, or shared Wired?

- Others as strangers in the Lobby: you see them, they're real.
- Asymmetric: one player in the real world, one in the Wired, each sees half.
- "Everyone's connected" fits shared Wired. But story is harder to control.

## Open questions

1. Solo story game, or multiplayer?
2. How much danger: pure exploration, stealth, or something that can hurt you?
3. Tone: melancholic mystery, or outright horror?
4. What does the player *do* minute to minute, in one sentence?
5. How does a session end: a case solved, a cycle survived, a day passing?

## Decision log

- 2026-10-19: Tone is **melancholic mystery with dread**, not outright horror. Why: matches Lain; strangeness and loneliness over scares.
- 2026-10-19: **Solo or small co-op** (2 to 4), not a 64-player shared Wired. Why: story control. Co-op idea: one player in the real world, one in the Wired.
- 2026-10-19: **Light danger.** Threats unsettle and cost you (presence, memory, access), they don't kill you.
- Leaning: spine = **A (Investigator) + C (Presence as resource)**, with B (two-world cause and effect) as the puzzle language.
