# The Living Wired: toys and activities (what is actually fun)

Companion to [living-wired.md](living-wired.md). Story is on hold. This file is about **what players do with their hands and with each other**, and how we find out whether it is fun.

## The rule: toys first

A social world is fun when it has **toys**: things that are fun to do for 30 seconds with no goal, are better with friends, and leave something others can see. Story, mystery and progression sit on top of toys. If the toys are dull, nothing else saves the game.

### What makes a toy worth building

| Test | Question |
| --- | --- |
| **Alone** | Is it fun for 30 seconds with no instructions? |
| **Together** | Is it better with one more person? |
| **Leaves a trace** | Does it make something others can see, hear or find? |
| **Cheap** | Can one developer build a first version in days, not months? |
| **Fits** | Is it human-made, bot-free and Lain-flavored? |

## The toy list

Scored 1 to 3 against each test (higher is better). **Cost** is rough build effort: **S** days, **M** a couple of weeks, **L** a month or more.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## The toys in detail

### 1. Movement and presence

**Fantasy:** you are a signal drifting through a place, not a person walking a corridor.

- **Walk and run:** normal first person, a touch floaty.
- **Glide:** hold jump to float slowly, steer gently. Crossing a Plaza by gliding feels good and lets you look at things from above.
- **Rails:** cables and conduits are rails you can ride (hold interact to snap on, release to leave). They are the fast way through a district.
- **Dive:** step into a link (a door, a port, an address ring) and the screen **dives**: a short, stylised transition (about 0.8 seconds) into the next place. This is the browsing experience, so it must feel great.
- **Phase:** pass through thin walls marked with a faint shimmer, for shortcuts and secrets.
- **Emotes:** a quick wheel of about eight: nod, wave, static pulse, glitch wave, sit, point, shrug, the phrase "present day, present time."
- **Sit:** sit on chairs, edges and ledges. Sitting is the way to say "I am here, talk to me."

**Fun test:** do players cross a room by gliding when they could walk? Do they ride the rails more than once?

### 2. Marks

**Fantasy:** you leave a trace of yourself on the world, and others find it.

- **Spray:** hold a button to draw on a surface (walls, floors, glass). Brush size and a few colors from the palette.
- **Stamp:** a set of small stamps (hearts, glitch icons, tiny faces, arrows, eyes) with one press.
- **Note:** write a short line (up to 40 characters) that appears as a small sticker.
- **Footprint:** automatic, a faint trace of where you walked, visible only up close.
- **Decay:** marks fade over days unless the place's owner keeps them. Public places clear faster.
- **Mark limits:** a per-place cap and a cooldown, so no spam wall.
- **Review:** a place owner can clear marks. Anyone can report.

**Fun test:** do players draw things unprompted, and do others react, copy, or reply with their own?

### 3. Artifacts

**Fantasy:** an idea becomes an object you can hold, throw and show.

- **What they are:** a small image, a short sound, a few lines of text, or a tiny loop, shown as a small physical object (a tape, a disk, a postcard, a glowing cube).
- **Verbs:** pick up, carry (up to 3), throw, drop, place on a table, give to a person.
- **Inspect:** hold an artifact up and it plays or opens (the image fills a frame, the sound plays, the text scrolls).
- **Make one:** from your Navi, import an image or sound (converted to the lo-fi style) or write text. It appears in your hands.
- **Marked with the maker and the history** (who made it, who has held it).
- **Show and tell tables** in Plazas where people put artifacts down for others.

**Fun test:** do players make and throw things at each other? Do they gather around a table?

### 4. Remix bench (later)

- Put two artifacts on a bench and press combine. A few simple rules: image plus sound makes a clip, image plus text makes a poster, two sounds make a layered loop.
- The result is a new artifact crediting both makers.
- **Human-made:** only combines what people made. No generation.
- **Fun test:** do people trade ingredients to see what comes out?

### 5. Tuning

**Fantasy:** a dial in a quiet room, scanning through static for voices.

- **The device:** a physical radio on your desk and one in every Plaza. A real dial you turn with the mouse.
- **What you hear:** broadcasts made by people: music, talk, readings, odd noise, signals from dead servers, scraps of a rumor. Static between them.
- **Broadcast:** a player can run a station from a mic or a small queue of artifacts, for as long as they are online. A live broadcast shows up on the dial.
- **Discover:** a few stations are hidden at odd frequencies and found only by turning slowly. Stations can be saved to presets.
- **Listen together:** anyone near the radio hears the same station, so a group can tune together.
- **Traces:** a station you tuned to leaves your name on its listener list. Hosts see how many people heard them.

**Fun test:** do people turn the dial for minutes? Do they stop and tell others "come hear this"?

### 6. Party rounds

**Fantasy:** a short, silly game when five people are standing around, run from a table in a Plaza.

Four rounds, 3 to 8 players, 3 to 5 minutes each:

| Round | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| **Garble** | One player writes a sentence. The next sees only noise-degraded text and rewrites it, passing it along. At the end the original and the garbled result are shown side by side. Funny and on theme (the Noise). |
| **Co-draw** | Each player draws one section of a shared page without seeing the rest, then it is revealed. |
| **Caption** | A random artifact appears. Everyone writes a caption. Everyone votes. |
| **Whose page** | Players guess which of them wrote an anonymous page or poem from a pool. |

- Started from a table, with a host or no host.
- Results can be saved as artifacts.
- **No bots:** all content is player-written.

**Fun test:** do people ask to play another round?

### 7. Caches (later)

- **Hide:** put an artifact at a location (a Cell corner, a hidden address, a dead server) with a short clue.
- **Find:** someone solves the clue and finds it. They can sign a log in the cache.
- **Return:** the hider sees who found it and when.
- Works asynchronously, so one player can hide something and come back to a log.
- **Fun test:** do people hide things for friends, and do the finders brag?

### 8. Cell decorating

- **Place props:** pick up a prop from a shelf and drop it in the room. It snaps to surfaces, with a slight physics settle.
- **Props library:** about 40 at first (bed, shelf, lamp, plants, floppies, cables, fan, posters, an old TV).
- **Rotate and scale** by a fixed set of steps. No free scale, to keep coherence.
- **Light and window** pickers. **Save** and **share**.
- **Visit mode:** friends can sit on your bed, play your radio, read your terminal.
- **Fun test:** do players spend more than ten minutes arranging a room, and do friends visit it?

### 9. Rig building (later)

- A tactile mini-game on the desk in your real-world room: place a case, then slot a board, memory, storage and a power supply. Route cables. Press the power button.
- **The boot sequence is the payoff:** fans spin, lights flicker, the screen draws a boot log, a short chime.
- Better parts change what Navi can do (more storage, more bandwidth).
- **Fun test:** is the first boot a moment people clip and share?

### 10. Light intrusion (later)

The Navi virtual computer used for exploring dead machines: recon, logins, read the logs, find what was left. It is already built; the work is a small set of puzzles and a few machines.

## What I would cut or hold

- **Combat.** Not needed.
- **Complex economy.** Hold. Attention and artifacts are enough.
- **A big avatar creator.** Hold. A few looks and a persona name.
- **Free building.** Hold. Kits and props are enough.
- **Anything that only works at high population.** Prefer toys that work with 1 to 6 people.

## First prototype: the toy box

**One room** (a Plaza-sized lo-fi space) with:

1. Glide, rails and emotes (toy 1).
2. Marks on the walls and floor (toy 2).
3. Artifacts on a table, with pick up, throw and inspect (toy 3).
4. A radio with five handmade stations (toy 5, the simplest version).

No Navi, no backend, no persistence. Everything resets. It runs for 2 to 8 players on one server.

### How to test whether it is fun

Run it with 4 to 6 friends for 20 minutes. **Give no instructions** beyond how to move. Watch and note:

| Question | Pass |
| --- | --- |
| Did they use at least three of the four toys without being told? | Yes |
| Did anyone say "come look at this" or "listen to this"? | At least twice |
| Did two people make something together without being asked? | At least once |
| Did someone laugh out loud? | Yes |
| After 20 minutes, did they stay when told it was over? | Most |
| What did they do the most, and what did they ignore? | Write it down |

If this fails, change the toys, not the story. If it passes, add one more toy and repeat.

## After the toy box (order)

1. Party rounds (toy 6) in the same room.
2. Cell decorating and visiting (toy 8).
3. Persistence of marks and artifacts (a first backend).
4. Pages as places (the main pitch).
5. Caches and the remix bench.
6. The rest.

## Open questions

1. Is gliding the right default, or does it feel like a gimmick? Try both.
2. What is the best size for a Plaza so people meet without being crowded (about 20 by 20 m for 8 players)?
3. How does Marks decay avoid both clutter and loss of memory?
4. Do Artifacts need a limit per player, and how do we stop the same image spamming everyone?
5. Which party rounds need a host, and which can run unattended?
6. What are the five first radio stations (music, a reading, odd noise, a rumor, a hidden one)?
