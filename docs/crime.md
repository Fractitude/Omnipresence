# Crime: grow, press, ship, get paid

The first playable slice of the crime game, as built. Grow a drug (**Static** or **Haze**), press it into tabs or vials, buy seeds in the **Hush** shop, take an order in the **Hush** app, pack a parcel at the packing table, post it from your mailbox, and get paid from escrow in **crypto** with a review. Cash out through the Exchange. Everything is rough placeholder gameplay: no minigames, no police, no disputes. The plan and the reasons are in [design-doc/crime-rp-first-build.md](design-doc/crime-rp-first-build.md); the three currencies are in [currency.md](currency.md).

```
Hush Shop: buy seeds with crypto ──► in transit ──► waits in the Mailbox ──► collect
seed ──plant (planting panel: choose the drug)──► Grow Tray ──wait (game clock)──► harvest ──► raw + a seed back
raw ──press (pressing panel: choose a batch)──► Press Bench ──► the drug's product (a batch with a quality)
Hush: accept an order ──► Packing table: pick the order and a batch of its product (any quality) ──► Mailbox: send all parcels
Mailman collects on the clock ──► in transit ──► delivered ──► buyer reviews ──► escrow releases
the buyer rates it by their personality, the quality against the ask, and how late it was
escrow pays crypto (minus a fee by reputation) ──► Hush Exchange: crypto ──► dollars (fee, delay)
```

## The loop

1. **Start.** A new player gets each drug's `StartSeeds` the first time their crime state is made: 2 Static cultures and no Haze seedlings. More come from the Shop: buy them in Hush with crypto, then **collect** them from the mailbox once they have arrived.
2. **Plant.** Use an empty grow tray and pick a drug in the planting panel (a seed of it must be in the bag). It is ready the drug's `GrowSeconds` later on the `WorldClock` (Static 10, Haze 25), so time offline counts. The crop quality is picked at random when planting (placeholder).
3. **Harvest.** Gives the drug's `HarvestRaw` raw (Static 12, Haze 8) and a seed back, so the loop can go on.
4. **Press.** Use a press bench and press a raw batch in the panel. Raw becomes the product at `RawPerProduct` raw each (Static 1 raw per tab, Haze 2 raw per vial; 8 Haze raw make 4 vials, an odd leftover stays in the bag). Quality = the batch's nudged by up to +/-`PressQualitySpread` (placeholder for the Tuning minigame).
5. **Orders.** In Hush the board shows 3 to 5 NPC orders for either drug (handle, note, count and product, like "5 x Haze vials", **asked quality**, price). Each buyer has a hidden **personality** (see Review): a Connoisseur asks for 10 more quality and pays 15 points more of price spread; the others ask as listed. The ask is a guide for the rating, not a requirement. **Accept** takes it off the board, reserves it for you and holds its payment in escrow. You have `DeliverWindowSeconds` to deliver; at most 5 orders at once.
6. **Pack.** Use the packing table at home (not Hush) and pick an accepted order and one of your batches in the panel. Any quality can be packed; the batch must be of the order's kind (its drug's product) and hold at least the ordered count. The host takes exactly the ordered count from that batch and gives one **parcel** item that remembers the order, the batch's quality and what is inside.
7. **Send.** Use **Send all parcels** at the mailbox: every parcel in the bag goes in one use, up to `MaxParcels` in the post at once (the rest stay in the bag, and the message says so). Any player can post any parcel they carry; the sender who packed it is the one delivered for and paid (see "Parcels: who posts, who is paid"). A parcel that cannot be posted stays in the bag and the poster is told why. The first sender or collector claims the box (remembered in the save). The flag goes up. At the next collection time the mailman takes them (Shipments: "Collected, in transit"), and `TransitSeconds` later they are delivered.
8. **Review and escrow.** On delivery the buyer rates the batch (1 to 5 stars, see "The rating rule") and escrow releases `EscrowSeconds` after the parcel arrived (also when you were away at the time). The agreed price is always paid in full; only the rating (and so reputation and the fee level) changes with quality and lateness. You get the price minus a fee by reputation (10 percent Newcomer, 7 Known after 3 good trades, 4 Trusted after 10 good trades and a rating of 4 or more). A good trade counts. **Teams:** the crypto is paid through `OrgPayouts.Distribute` (see [organizations.md](organizations.md)): if the player who packed the parcel is in an organization, each teammate with a share gets that percent of the payment at once and the packer keeps the rest.
9. **Late.** The deadline only governs **sending**. An order still Accepted or Packed (not yet in the mailbox) at its deadline is cancelled: a bad trade, a 1-star review, and a parcel still in your bag is taken. Once sent it is never cancelled by the clock; a parcel that arrives after the deadline is still paid for, but the buyer rates it lower and says in the review that it took too long.
10. **Exchange.** Hush changes money at the darknet Exchange: sell crypto for dollars at 1 to 1 minus 8 percent and the dollars arrive 2 minutes later; buy crypto with dollars at 1 to 1 minus 4 percent, at once. The Government Exchange (the Payments app) is cheaper and pays at once; see [currency.md](currency.md).

## The rating rule

`CrimeCatalog.Stars`. A buyer's score starts at `BaseStars` (3) plus the personality's bias. Let `delta` = quality delivered minus quality asked.

- Shortfall (`delta` < 0): lose one star per `ShortfallPerStar` points beyond the personality's `Tolerance`.
- Excess (`delta` > 0): gain `delta * ExcessWeight / PointsPerStar` stars (`PointsPerStar` = 7.5).
- Lateness: no penalty if the parcel arrives by the deadline. Late loses 1 star, plus 1 more for every further `LateStepSeconds` (300) late, at most `LateMaxStarsLost` (3), times the personality's `LateWeight` (rounded down). An on-time parcel can earn an Impatient buyer `EarlyBonus` if it arrives within `EarlyFraction` (25 percent) of the delivery window.
- The score is rounded, may move one star at random if the personality has noise, is capped by its `MaxStars` (a Skeptic gives 5 only at a raw score of 5.5 or more), and kept between its `MinStars` and 5. The review line is hand-written, by personality and stars; a late parcel gets a line that says it took too long.

| Personality | Weight | Ask / price | Tolerance | Points per star short | Excess weight | Bias | Noise | Late weight | Early bonus | Min / Max stars |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Layman | 35 | 0 / 0 | 20 | 15 | 2.0 | 0 | 20% of 1 star | 1 | 0 | 1 / 5 |
| Connoisseur | 20 | +10 / +15 | 0 | 8 | 0.5 | 0 | none | 1 | 0 | 1 / 5 |
| Impatient | 15 | 0 / 0 | 0 | 7.5 | 1.0 | 0 | none | 2 | +1 | 1 / 5 |
| Easygoing | 15 | 0 / 0 | 10 | 7.5 | 1.0 | +1 | none | 0.5 | 0 | 3 / 5 |
| Skeptic | 15 | 0 / 0 | 0 | 7.5 | 1.0 | -1 | none | 1 | 0 | 1 / 4 |

The personality is host-side data on the market order and the saved `OrderRecord`, never synced and never in the Hush state or the packing panel. It shows only in the sound of the buyer's note. An old save with only the old buyer type is moved on load (`Sanitize`): Honest becomes Easygoing, Picky becomes Connoisseur.

**To add a drug:** add a `ProductDef` to `BuildProducts` (three `ItemDef`s with new ids and a `Category` that is filled in from the name) and the three item id constants to `ItemCatalog`. Texts that name the product come from the definition; a drug with its own flavour (buyer notes, a use effect) is code on top of this.

## Combining and splitting stacks

Items stack only up to their kind's `MaxStack` (256 for the drugs' items). Hold a key while dragging a stack in the Character menu inventory or in the storage panel (bag or box, in every direction: bag to box, box to bag, inside each). The keys are read when the mouse is released (`StackDrag.Held`: Shift or Ctrl from the drop event, else `Input.Keyboard.Down( "SHIFT" )` / `"CTRL"`, and the right-hand keys). The dragged icon shows a label ("Combine", "Split half", "Split half and combine", or what is wrong) and a slot is marked as a target only when the drop would work.

| Keys | Dropped on | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| none | an empty or occupied slot | A plain move; an occupied slot swaps (as before). |
| **Shift** | a stack of the same kind | **Combine.** The target gets both counts, up to the kind's max stack (256); what does not fit stays in the dragged slot, and a full target says "That stack is full. A stack holds at most 256." The dragged slot is empty afterwards. |
| **Ctrl** | an empty slot | **Split.** Half of the stack (`Count / 2`, rounded down: 12 gives 6 and 6, 5 gives a moved 2 and 3 left) goes to a new stack there, with the same kind and quality. A stack of 1 cannot be split. |
| Ctrl | an occupied slot | Nothing happens (no swap, no merge). |
| **Ctrl + Shift** | a stack of the same kind | Half of the dragged stack is added to the target (the half has the dragged stack's quality); the dragged stack keeps the other half. Onto an empty slot it is a plain split. |
| Shift | an empty slot | Nothing happens. |

- **Quality** of a combined stack is the average of the two weighted by the quantity added, rounded to the nearest (halves round up): 12 at quality 40 and 8 at quality 70 make 20 at quality 52. A plain mean would be wrong for stacks of different sizes. When only part fits under the cap, the weights are the target's count and the count that moved.
- **Combinable kinds:** exactly those with `MaxStack` above 1 (`ItemCatalog.IsCombinable`): the drugs' seeds, raw materials and products. Parcels and ordinary items cannot be combined or split. Two different kinds never combine (a raw never mixes into a product).
- **Where the rules live:** `StackRules.Plan` (`Code/Inventory/StackRules.cs`) is the one place that does the arithmetic and works out both resulting slots without changing anything. `PlayerInventory.Stack( from, to, mode )` (the bag; on a client it asks the host with `HostStack( from, to, mode )`, which checks the caller owns the bag) and `StorageRules.Stack` (any two slots of the bag and the box, asked by `StorageBox.RequestStack( fromSide, from, toSide, to, mode )`) plan, check, and only then write both slots, so the total is kept exactly and a refusal changes nothing. `mode` is a `StackMode` (1 combine, 2 split, 3 both); anything else is refused. The box request goes through the same `Begin()` checks as the other moves (record loaded, reach, `CanUse`). Only slot indices, a side and the mode travel; the kinds, counts and qualities are the host's.
- **Saved:** the bag's batches are copied into `CrimeRecord.Items` on every inventory change and the box lives in `CrimeRecord.Storage`, so combined and split stacks are saved and restored like any batch (`Sanitize` clamps qualities to 100). The bag's slot positions are not kept across a restart, only the batches. Packing takes exactly the ordered count from a stack of at least that many (`TakeFrom`), so a combined stack works for `Pack` and the parcel keeps the stack's averaged quality; the rest stays in the slot at that quality.

## Parcels: who posts, who is paid

A parcel is like a real one: the sender and the receiver are written on it. A parcel can be dropped and picked up by another player (`docs/world-items.md`), so whoever carries it can post it, and **it is delivered and the player who packed it for the order is paid**, never the poster.

- **What is on the parcel** (`ItemData`, plain JSON, additive: old items load with the defaults): `SenderId` (the packer's Steam id, host-written), `SenderName` (display name, shown as "From:"), `To` (the buyer's handle), `OrderId`, `Contents`, and a description of the order: `OrderKind`, `OrderCount`, `OrderPrice` (agreed total), `OrderDeadline`. They are written once, by `PlayerCrime.Pack` (`ParcelPostRules.Stamp`). The buyer's personality is deliberately **not** on it (it is synced with a dropped item and is a host secret). A player's own parcels packed before this existed get stamped with their id when the bag is restored; a parcel with no sender left (one in a storage box, packed before this) is posted against the poster's own orders as before.
- **Source of truth:** the **sender's `OrderRecord`**. `ParcelPostRules.Judge` checks the order exists in the sender's record, is `Packed` (so a duplicate parcel, a delivered or paid order, a cancelled one is refused: nothing is ever paid twice), is not past its deadline, and that the parcel's kind, count and contents match the record. The price, count, quality (`PackedQuality`), deadline and personality are all read from the order record, never from the parcel; the parcel's numbers are a description and a consistency check. A forged number on a parcel changes nothing it is paid.
- **Posting:** `PlayerCrime.SendAllParcels` takes each parcel from the poster's bag only when it is judged shippable, then `ParcelPostRules.Ship` moves the sender's order Packed -> Shipped and makes the shipping `ParcelRecord` **in the sender's record** (it shows in the sender's Hush Shipments). From there it is the same `CrimeRules.Advance` as for an own parcel: collection time from the post clock at posting time, transit, the buyer's review (lateness against the order's deadline, dice seeded by sender and order), escrow, the fee by the sender's reputation, the crypto lands in the sender's record, and the sender's reputation and good trade count change. The deadline counts toward when it was **sent** (a Packed order past its deadline is refused, and the sender's own clock would cancel it).
- **Whose record:** a sender who is playing: their live record (`ProgressCache.Peek`, the one `PlayerCrime` is attached to), so there is one source of truth and no stale file copy, and their `PlayerCrime` is told (flag, views, a message "X posted your parcel for Y."). A sender who is away: the record is in the cache, or is read first (`PlayerCrime.PostWhenLoadedAsync`; the parcel stays in the bag until it is there and is found again by what it is, so nothing is lost if the poster drops it meanwhile), then saved at once and the offline processor is woken (`OfflineCrimeProcessor.Wake`), which delivers and pays on the clock like for any absent player. A sender id that has no record or no such order is simply refused ("Nobody is waiting for this parcel any more."). The sender is not messaged when away (there is no while-away log yet).
- **The poster's limits:** the in-post cap `CrimeCatalog.MaxParcels` counts the poster's **own** parcels in the post plus the parcels they posted for others that have not arrived (`CrimeRecord.Posted`, a `PostedRecord` per parcel: id, sender, arrival time; entries are forgotten after the arrival time, no clock tick needed). So a poster cannot flood the post, and nobody can flood a sender's post: only that sender's `Packed` orders (at most `MaxActiveOrders`) can ship, once each. There is no postage cost in the game yet; when there is, it is the poster's. The mailman's rhythm is the same for everyone.
- **Messages:** the poster hears "You posted a parcel for <buyer>. It will be delivered." and nothing of the sender's money or reputation; a refused parcel says why in a sentence (no order, already posted, too late, does not match) and stays in the bag.
- **Persistence:** the sender fields go through the bag save (`CrimeRecord.Items`), boxes (`StorageRules.CleanStacks` calls `ItemData.CopySenderFrom`) and a drop's JSON. Any code that rebuilds an `ItemData` field by field from a file must call `CopySenderFrom` for a parcel, or it loses the sender.

## What is saved

`ProgressRecord.Crime` (`CrimeRecord`), in the player's progress file, record version 3:

- **Stacks above their kind's size on load** (a 9999 stack saved before the cap, or after the cap is lowered) are split, not clamped: into full stacks plus a remainder (9999 tabs become 39 x 256 + 15). The bag restore (`PlayerCrime.Restore`) puts them into as many slots as it takes, one stack at a time, and logs a warning for what does not fit; `CrimeRecord.Sanitize` splits `Items` (`StorageRules.CleanStacks`); in a box the extra stacks take the lowest free slot indexes of the same box, up to 96, beyond the box's visible size if it is full (they stay reachable), and a warning is logged only if all 96 are used. A total in one saved batch is still cut to `ItemCatalog.MaxCount` (9999), which is also the sanity bound kept for counts that are not stacks (order, delivery and parcel-contents counts).
- A parcel's `To` and sender facts (`SenderId`, `SenderName`, `OrderKind`, `OrderCount`, `OrderPrice`, `OrderDeadline`) are saved and synced like its `OrderId`, cut short on load. `Posted` lists the parcels the player posted for others that have not arrived.
- A parcel's `To` is saved and synced like its `OrderId`, cut to 64 characters on load. A parcel saved before `To` existed gets it from its order's handle when the record loads (bag and boxes, in `Sanitize`); if the order is gone it has none and no "To:" line.
- `Items`: the batches in the inventory, copied from it whenever it changes and put back when the player's crime state starts. (The inventory itself is not saved; ordinary picked-up items are still lost on restart.)
- A parcel item keeps its `Contents` (capped at 20 entries, counts and qualities clamped on load; an unknown kind shows by its stored name; an old parcel has none and shows no lines).
- `Deliveries`: what was bought in the Shop (kind, count, quality, `ArriveAt`, in transit or in the mailbox). Old saves have none.
- Each accepted order keeps its `Personality` and the `PackedQuality`; `MinQuality` is the asked quality.
- `Trays` (by the tray object's id; each has a `Kind`, the drug planted. A tray saved before there were two drugs has none and loads as Static, as does an unknown id), `Orders` (accepted, with state), `Parcels`, `Cashouts`, `Reviews` (last 20), reputation counters, `MailboxId`, `Started`.
- Not saved: the open orders on the board. They start fresh at every start (what a player accepted is in their record).
- Next to the records (`progress/`): `_pending.json`, the ids of players with work waiting on the clock; each file also has a `.bak` copy of its previous write. In the data folder: `mailboxes.json` (which player owns which mailbox), `world.json` (the game clock) and `world_items.json` (the dropped items, see `docs/world-items.md`).

Old saves load: the section defaults to empty, and `Sanitize` clamps everything.

## When a player is away

Orders in the post, shop deliveries, cash-outs and unsent orders keep going while a player is not playing, and the server remembers them across a restart. Everything is applied to the player's own saved record.

- **Who runs it.** `OfflineCrimeSystem` (a `GameObjectSystem`, host only) drives an `OfflineCrimeProcessor` once a second. For every player with work waiting it loads the record through `PlayerProgression` and runs `CrimeRules.Advance` at the clock's time: parcels are collected and delivered, the buyer's review is written, escrow pays crypto, a cash-out lands, a shop delivery reaches the mailbox, an unsent order is cancelled at its deadline (a bad trade and a 1-star review, as always), hardware shipments arrive. It saves the record at once. It serves a player who left, a player who is dead and has not respawned, and a player the server has not seen since it started.
- **The same rules everywhere.** A player who comes back is caught up by the same call, so the result does not depend on who ran it or how often. Every event happens at its own time: the review is stamped with the delivery time, escrow releases at the delivery plus `EscrowSeconds`, releases run in the order they fall due (the fee depends on the good trades so far), a cancel is stamped with the deadline. The buyer's dice are seeded by player and order, so a replay gives the same stars. A payment the wallet cannot hold waits and is tried again a minute (game time) later.
- **One record, no race.** `ProgressCache` holds one in-memory record per player. A player's `PlayerProgress` attaches to it from the moment it is loaded and detaches when the object goes; the processor never changes an attached record, and checks that at the moment it changes one. Both run on the main thread and each step is synchronous, so a player who connects while the processor works sees the whole result.
- **Which players.** `progress/_pending.json` lists the ids with work waiting. An id is written there before its record is saved with work in it, and leaves after a save with none, so a crash leaves at most one extra look. At start, the processor reads it; if it is missing or damaged it looks at every record in `progress/` once and rebuilds it (`offline_rescan` does that by hand). A record that can't be read is logged and tried again after a minute.
- **The bag.** A parcel in the bag of a player who is away is in `CrimeRecord.Items`; when its order is cancelled the processor removes it there, so `PlayerCrime.Restore` finds it gone.
- **Mailboxes.** A mailbox learns its owner from `mailboxes.json` when it starts, so a player who is away keeps their box after a restart. The processor keeps the flag of the owner's box right (up while a parcel waits for the mailman).
- **Time.** The game clock stops while the server is off (`world.json` holds the time of the last save), so downtime costs nobody a deadline. It is saved at least every 10 seconds and with every record save.
- **Saving.** A change to an attached record is saved 2 seconds after the last change, and at the latest 10 seconds after the first (`PlayerProgress`). The processor saves at once. A file is written as `.bak` first and the damaged file falls back to it, so a write cut short costs at most the last write. The `OfflineCrimeSystem` writes every record in memory when the scene goes (a clean stop, a scene change); a crash loses at most the changes of the last 2 seconds (10 under a constant stream of changes), and a record replays from the older state with the same dice. Stopping the editor was seen to write the record at once.
- **Testing without touching a real save.** `progress_test_data on` (before the player has spawned) keeps progress in `progress_test/`, the clock in `world_test.json`, the mailbox claims in `mailboxes_test.json` and the storage claims in `storage_test.json`. `offline_away` takes your player object away without disconnecting, `offline_back` respawns you, `offline_status` shows what the processor looks after, and `world_time_skip_minutes` moves the clock without a player. See [console-commands.md](console-commands.md).
- **Not done.** No "while you were away" summary for the player (the reviews and the money are simply there when they return). The open orders on the board are not saved.

## Security

Same rules as the apps in AGENTS.md.

- **The host decides everything.** `PlayerCrime` methods run on the host only. The tray's harvest and the mailbox are `Interactable` options that run on the host (the host re-checks the player's reach). The tray's Plant option, the press bench's option and the packing table's option are local (they only open a panel); planting, pressing and packing are the `RequestPlant`, `RequestPress` and `RequestPack` RPCs. Hush is a shared app: its logic runs in the host's copy, and the seat comes from the computer (`GameAppSeat`), never from an action.
- **Actions carry ids and amounts only.** `accept <orderId>`, `sell <amount>`, `buy <amount>`, `tab <name>`, `category <name>`, `status <name>`, `quantity <n>` and `shop <itemId> <quantity>`. Hush has no pack action. The planting panel sends a tray id and a drug id (`RequestPlant`: the host checks the caller, that the tray is within `GrowTray.Reach`, that the drug exists, that the tray is empty and that the seed is in the bag). The pressing panel sends an inventory slot index (`RequestPress`: the host checks the caller, a press bench within `PressBench.Reach`, and that the slot holds raw of a known drug). The packing panel sends an order id and an inventory slot index (`RequestPack`); the host takes the player from `Rpc.Caller`, checks a packing table is near, that the player owns the order, that it is Accepted and in time, and that the slot holds a batch of the right kind and count, and never reads a quality, count or price from the client. The shop takes the price and delivery time from `CrimeCatalog` and refuses an unknown item, a quantity outside 1 to `ShopMaxQuantity`, too many deliveries in transit, or not enough crypto. Filter names are checked against the catalogue and the status list. The price, count, quality, times and fees come from the host's records. An exchange amount is the player's own to give and is checked against their balance and a maximum.
- **Buyers' secrets stay on the host.** The buyer personality is not in the UI state, the packing panel or any synced data. Nothing that sends a message as an NPC exists yet (buyer notes are plain text in the order).
- **Credits are never touched.** Crypto and dollars change only in `Exchange`.
- **Parcels cannot be faked.** A parcel can be dropped and picked up (`docs/world-items.md`); only packing makes one, and only the host writes its sender. Clients send slot indices only. Whatever a parcel says, the sender's order record decides whether it ships and what is paid (see "Parcels: who posts, who is paid"). `crime_give` is editor only.
- **The offline processor has no input from a client.** It works on ids from the host's own index and store, one record at a time, never on a record a live player is attached to, and no RPC or app call reaches it. The editor-only `offline_*` commands run on the host and only in the editor.
- **Hush needs home and the network.** In the Wired it says "No signal"; offline it says so. The host checks the same again before acting.
- A client's mailbox claim is by Steam id on the host, and a box whose object is gone is let go.

## Placing things in a scene

`Assets/scenes/home.scene` has one of each placed through the editor (the scene change is not committed): **Grow Tray**, **Press Bench** and **Packing Table** at (-70, -50, 0) (from the prefabs in `Assets/crime/`), next to the existing **MailBox**. To place more by hand:

- **Grow tray / press bench / packing table:** drag the prefab in. Or make a GameObject with a model, a collider, an `Interactable` and a `GrowTray` (or `PressBench`, `PackingTable`) component; **Network Mode must be Object**, or the press never reaches the host. The options are added in code, so the `Interactable` can keep its default option.
- **Mailbox:** the existing `Assets/MailBox.prefab` works as is (it is a network object). The "Send all parcels" and "Collect parcels" options are added in code, and they are the only options: any other the object has is removed at run time, so the flag cannot be moved by hand.
- Each tray is its own tray in the save, by its object's id. Do not duplicate a placed tray and expect two to share state.

## Known gaps

- **Not tested on a second client.** The wallet and crime components reach clients by a network refresh made on the host; this was checked on the host (singleplayer) only. Check that a client sees its own cash and the tray labels.
- **The Hush panel was drawn and its text read, but not clicked** by a real mouse. Its view model and all the rules were driven directly.
- **Ordinary inventory items are still not saved**, only crime batches.
- **Dropped items are saved by the host** in `world_items.json` and come back at the next start (`docs/world-items.md`, "Saved between sessions"). Nothing wipes them on its own: a season wipe must call `WorldItemRegistry.Wipe`.
- No buyer messages in the Messages app yet (`NaviNpcs`), no Hot flag, no tutorial order, no sounds. Quality is a random placeholder. One home, one mailbox, the first claimant.
- Two drugs (Static, Haze); one order asks for one kind. Haze's use effect (Numb) waits for the consumables.
- A cash-out waits in the save until the wallet can hold it; the player is not told when it lands.
- Two players at one mailbox: the first sender owns it.
- A parcel posted for another player is shown in that player's Hush as an ordinary shipment; the sender is not told when they are away. The sender's mailbox flag goes up only for a parcel they posted from their own box (the flag follows the owner's waiting parcels).
- The live path of a sender who is playing (another connection) was checked through the rules and the code, not with a second client: only one player could be run. The away path was run end to end on test data.
